My Book Review: Chapter Analysis


The Richest Man in Babylon

by George Samuel Clason

Quick note before you start reading: I will be reflecting on two chapters only — “Seven Cures for a Lean Purse” and “Five Laws of Gold”.
I highly recommend you add it to your readling list! Click here for a PDF copy.


Chapter 3 - Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

So many of us search for the great breakthrough that will lead us to true wealth. This chapter has a very powerful message and is greatly beneficial to young people like myself. As my mom would always say, put a little aside regardless of the amount because you’ve got to break yourself into the habit of saving.

Growing up, I saw firsthand how poor financial decisions could impact your quality of life, as well as the fear of realizing that you’ve lived all your life and don’t have much to show for it. With this in mind, I’ve made it a point to educate myself on good financial practices, so this was definitely an appreciated read.

What I’ve gathered is that you have to set little or major milestones for yourself so that you can build wealth. The first step is to maintain discipline, “bind yuh belly,” as Jamaicans would say, and put aside a percentage of your pay before you spend from it. Live within your means, and as soon as you have a surplus of funds, invest, stay financially savvy, and secure other ways of making even more money.

Without a doubt, once something becomes a habit, it becomes seamless, and there is no change to be made unless you take the first step.


Chapter 5 - The Five Laws of Gold

I’m sure I’m not the only one who has been told countless times to invest your money in this and that. The truth is, the concept of investing is truly intimidating, so while it is fairly easy to dish out the advice, it’s a whole other ball game to actually get started. As the author explains in this chapter, it is a fool’s lot to venture down a path they know nothing of and choose to act without leaning on those who have already trodden the way.

This chapter emphasizes the importance of taking cautious measures when handling money and taking risks only when there is proof that the ROI will be greater in the long run.

I believe it is very important to avoid tunnel vision when one comes into money, as it is a dangerous thing to ignore broader financial concerns simply because you’re focused on the one you think you need to handle. One should always exercise discernment and analyze new opportunities before allocating funds towards them.

All in all, the author relays this information beautifully, which makes this book an easy read. I will definitely keep message in mind for future reference.
