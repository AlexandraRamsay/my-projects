About Me


Three things that get me through the semester

I'm a perfectionist. I kid you not, this doesn't always work in my favour. While it helps me fine-tune the smallest details, I can end up taking ridiculously long on projects, including this website. Besides forcing me to crunch when a deadline gets close, it has also helped me learn more while doing assignments, because I keep reworking them until I understand every part, not just enough to finish.

I'm disciplined. Or at least, I try my very best to be. University isn't fully catered to working students, so I've really had to juggle both a full-time job and school while keeping my grades up. More often than not, I've had to choose work over school and vice versa, and I'm not going to lie, it can be stressful. But I still show up for both, even on the days I'm completely drained. I'll study on my lunch breaks or chip away at an assignment throughout the day so I don't fall behind.

I read a lot. Mostly fiction, and most of it happens before the semester starts, which is funny because the semester is exactly when I should be making more time for it. I love a good book, and it's such a great feeling when you start one and realize it's exceeding your expectations! Reading helps me unwind after a long day or after hitting that submit button on an assignment that was almost my last straw. It's my way of resetting so I don't burn out.


My hero is my mother

You know that poem "For My Mother (May I Inherit Half Her Strength)"? That's what I think of when I think about my mom. Age 54, mother of four, Jill of all trades with a mind as sharp as a tack. Being a single mother never held her back, and her children wanted for nothing for as long as we lived under her roof. My mother is the definition of resilience, which is why I admire her so deeply.

As the queen of green thumbs, my mother tended a lavish rose garden for most of my childhood. My favourite memory is in that very garden, where we would sit and fill bags of dirt or pull weeds while playing word games or talking about whatever came to mind. She was always ready to give her two cents whether you thought you needed it or not, and once she started, there was really nothing to do but sit and listen.


Tool or crutch? Where I draw the line with AI

I believe, as students, it is our duty to make the most out of the resources available to us, but to also use these resources responsibly and ethically. AI has proved its purpose wonderfully during every study session, whether I use NotebookLM to generate podcasts from long PowerPoint presentations or when I need a topic broken down into a simple, step-by-step approach from the basics so I can understand it better. While I agree that there is a not-so-thick line between using AI as a tool and becoming solely dependent on it, there is still much to gain from using it. AI can be used to facilitate interactive learning sessions and improve teaching pedagogies, which would ultimately help produce higher-performing students (Nagelhout, 2025).

From my own experience as a learner, I also have to make a more conscious effort to use AI tools less because I can be honest with myself and tell when I am really not learning when I should be.

According to University Canada West, “overreliance on AI tools can lead to reduced critical thinking and problem-solving skills, as students may depend too heavily on automated assistance” (University Canada West, 2026). This is, by all means, true, which we can all agree on. AI should support learning, not make me lose brain cells in the process or affect my ability to solve problems on my own.
